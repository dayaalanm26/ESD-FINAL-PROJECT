GENERIC DSO | CH2 POTENTIOMETER AND PWM LED
==========================================

1. Wire the four inputs on your classic ESP32 DevKit / WROOM-32.

   CH1  Light AO or LDR divider junction -> GPIO32
   CH2  Potentiometer middle leg        -> GPIO35
   CH3  Sound AO / ENV                   -> GPIO33
   CH4  IR DO / OUT                      -> GPIO34

   For the 10 kΩ potentiometer: one outer leg to 3.3 V and the other
   outer leg to ESP32 GND. Connect all sensor grounds together.
   Use the regulated 3.3 V rail that powers your other sensor modules.
   If the board's 3V3 pin is blocked and GPIO26 is free, set
   POWER_10K_POT_FROM_GPIO26 = true in the sketch and connect the 10 kΩ
   pot's high-side outer leg to GPIO26. Keep this pin dedicated to the pot.
   Alternatively use an external regulated 3.3 V rail with its ground
   joined to ESP32 GND. Keep supply outputs separate.
   Wire GPIO25 through a 1 kΩ resistor to the LED anode (+), then
   connect the LED cathode (-) to ESP32 GND.
   Remove any old PWM divider from GPIO34 before connecting IR.
   For ADS1115 wiring and other details, see docs/FOUR_SENSOR_WIRING.txt.

2. Open and upload this sketch in Arduino IDE:

   firmware/ESP32_Potentiometer_CH2/ESP32_Potentiometer_CH2.ino

   Select your ESP32 board and its COM port. The sketch uses the
   ESP32 board package's built-in Arduino and Wire libraries.
   Close Arduino Serial Monitor and Serial Plotter.

3. In the extracted Generic_DSO folder, run Start_DSO_Conda.bat. It uses
   your portable-dso environment and installs any missing libraries.
   If the launcher cannot find Conda, open your Conda PowerShell and run
   .\Start_DSO_Conda.bat from the same folder.
   If the environment is missing, create it once:
     conda create -n portable-dso python=3.13 pip
   For regular Python installations use Start_DSO.bat.

4. Select Serial, the ESP32 COM port, 921600 baud, and Connect.
   The sensor preset starts capture in Roll. New samples enter at the
   left edge and older samples move continuously toward the right edge.
   The time labels show how long ago samples arrived. Hold with Stop.
   Plot view: select CH2 Potentiometer, All stacked, or All overlaid.

Turn the knob. CH2 shows measured wiper voltage and the LED brightens
as the voltage rises. If clockwise rotation lowers the voltage, swap
the two outer legs and leave the wiper on GPIO35. For the actual 1 kHz
PWM pulses, put the physical oscilloscope probe tip on GPIO25 and its
ground clip on ESP32 GND. Start at 500 µs/div and a rising-edge trigger.

If the app says Waiting for ESP32, confirm which COM port disappears
when unplugged and reappears when reconnected. Keep 921600 selected and
press the ESP32 EN/RESET button once. The new sketch repeats hello.
To check serial output in Arduino Serial Monitor, disconnect the DSO
first, select 921600, and close the monitor before reconnecting the DSO.
